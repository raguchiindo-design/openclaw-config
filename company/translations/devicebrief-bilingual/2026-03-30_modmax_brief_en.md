---

The electric ATV market is witnessing a Chinese challenger: ModMax (魔迈), a Shenzhen-based startup founded by Lin Ji, former product vice president at Segway-Ninebot and general manager of its innovation division. The company has closed multi-million-dollar seed and angel rounds led by Hillhouse Capital, Yunshi Capital, and Yunmu Capital, with funds earmarked for R&D, team expansion, and international brand building.

Lin brings a proven track record of creating hit products—from Ninebot kart series and GT scooters to E-bikes—generating hundreds of millions in cumulative revenue. His core team also includes talent from Ninebot and DJI, companies known for disruptive hardware innovation.

## The $10B Market Opportunity

Off-road vehicles (ATVs/UTVs) have long been dominated by U.S. and Japanese giants—Polaris, Bombardier, Honda, and Yamaha—which together control over 97% of certain segments. Yet the market is ripe for disruption: electric powertrains offer zero emissions, low noise, and new form factors that legacy players have been slow to adopt.

Polaris, for instance, didn't release its first electric UTV until 2023, and performance lagged behind expectations. This gap opens a window for new entrants like ModMax.

## "Modular" as the Key Differentiator

ModMax's core innovation lies in its modular hardware architecture and software-defined approach. Instead of simply replacing a combustion engine with a motor (the "油改电" approach), the company has designed a highly redundant, extensible platform where users can swap functional modules as easily as building with Lego.

Whether the use case is forest hunting, desert crossing, or farm work, the vehicle adapts. This flexibility stems from decoupling traditional mechanical constraints—electric drivetrains enable both silent operation for agricultural settings and explosive performance for sport riding.

At the technical level, electric motors' instant torque can cause wheel spin on loose surfaces. ModMax tackled this with a proprietary control algorithm that delivers linear traction, balancing quiet efficiency with aggressive performance.

## China's EV Supply Chain Advantage

The broader trend favors Chinese hardware innovators. China produces over 70% of the world's lithium batteries and leads in motor control and autonomous driving algorithms. These strengths are now flowing into niche segments like ATVs.

Industry watchers expect the next 3–5 years to be a critical growth window for electric off-road vehicles. As companies like ModMax and Chilong Dynamics launch products, the decades-old dominance of U.S. and Japanese firms could finally be challenged.

Lin puts it this way: "Electrification isn't just about changing the power source—it revolutionizes the entire product logic. When a vehicle moves from 'functional fulfillment' to 'experience reconstruction,' and when users can customize like playing with Lego, we're opening an entirely new market."
